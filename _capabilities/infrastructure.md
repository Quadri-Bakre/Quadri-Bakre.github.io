## How I approach it
1. **Plan before the first screw.** Cable paths, rack space and power distribution are mapped out before the build starts.
2. **Build off-site.** Racks are built and dressed in the workshop, and switches are pre-configured before they go to site.
3. **Separate power and data.** Power runs down one side and data, audio and control down the other, with high and low voltage kept apart.
4. **Terminate and test every cable.** Pass-through connectors so the wiring order can be checked before crimping, and every data cable tested and certified, with a report for the client.
5. **Label everything.** Every device, cable and port, using a consistent scheme such as rack, device, port (AVR-01.SW-01.P08).
6. **Leave room to service it.** Brush plates, blanking panels, airflow, and space to add equipment later.

## Why I work this way
A rack is the part of the system the next engineer has to live with. I've had to strip back and rebuild racks left with no labels and no structure, and every hour spent untangling one is time the client pays for. Clean dressing, labelling and tested terminations make faults quick to find and changes safe to make, and building off-site means less time on site and fewer surprises.

## Tools I use
Trends Networks PoE Pro cable tester, Ideal pass-through crimper, impact punch-down tool, Brother P-Touch labeller, Fluke multimeter.

## Hardware I work with
Cisco switches, Excel patch panels, GUDE power distribution, lacing bars, brush plates.
