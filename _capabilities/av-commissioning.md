## How I approach it
1. **Build and test before site.** Racks are built, configured and commissioned at the workshop wherever possible, so faults are found on a bench, not in a finished home or on a yacht.
2. **Test against a written schedule.** Every system is checked against a structured test schedule rather than by eye: network services, audio streams, control, and recovery after power loss.
3. **Prove it under real conditions.** Dropout tests over time, switch power-cycle recovery, and checks on every floor or deck, not just in the rack room.
4. **Hand over properly.** As-built records, configuration backups and a topology drawing, and the client is shown how to use the system.

## Why I work this way
A system that works on the day isn't the same as a system that's been proven. Testing to a schedule means nothing depends on memory, every result is recorded, and the next engineer can see exactly what was checked. Finding problems in the workshop is far cheaper than finding them on site: on one yacht, a problem with the TV audio feed into the Dante amplifiers was found and fixed before anything went on board.

## Tools I use
Crestron Home Setup, Crestron Toolbox, Dante Controller, Netgear Engage, Afara (my own audit and reporting tool), written test schedules and as-built templates.
